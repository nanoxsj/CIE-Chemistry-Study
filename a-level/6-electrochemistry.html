6 Electrochemistry

Oxidation numbers, redox reactions and disproportionation

6 Electrochemistry

本章对应 CIE 9701 AS Chemistry 的 Electrochemistry，重点是用 oxidation numbers 和 electron transfer 判断并解释 redox reactions。

Learning objectives

学完本章后，你应该能够：

  • calculate oxidation numbers using the standard rules;
  • identify oxidation, reduction, oxidising agents and reducing agents;
  • recognise disproportionation reactions;
  • balance redox equations using changes in oxidation number;
  • explain halogen displacement and reactions with concentrated sulfuric acid;
  • use ionic equations and half-equations to explain electron transfer.

Key ideas

  • Oxidation number tracks electron transfer; it is not always the same as ionic charge in a covalent molecule.
  • Oxidation is an increase in oxidation number and reduction is a decrease; the oxidising agent is reduced and the reducing agent is oxidised.
  • In every balanced redox equation, total electrons lost equal total electrons gained.

6.1 Oxidation numbers

Core rules

The oxidation number of an uncombined element is zero. The sum of oxidation numbers in a neutral compound is zero; in a polyatomic ion it equals the charge of the ion.

Useful rules are:

  • a Group 1 metal is usually \(+1\) and a Group 2 metal is usually \(+2\);
  • fluorine is always \(-1\) in compounds;
  • oxygen is usually \(-2\), except in peroxides and compounds with fluorine;
  • hydrogen is usually \(+1\), but is \(-1\) in metal hydrides;
  • a simple ion has an oxidation number equal to its charge.

Worked example - Sulfate ion

Oxidation number of sulfur in \(\ce{SO4^{2-}}\)

The four oxygen atoms contribute \(4(-2)=-8\). The ion has charge \(-2\), so:

\[x-8=-2\qquad x=+6\]

Sulfur is therefore in oxidation state \(+6\).

6.2 Oxidation and reduction

Oxidation is loss of electrons and an increase in oxidation number. Reduction is gain of electrons and a decrease in oxidation number. Remember OIL RIG: oxidation is loss, reduction is gain.

The substance that is oxidised is the reducing agent, because it supplies electrons. The substance that is reduced is the oxidising agent, because it accepts electrons.

For example:

\[\ce{Mg(s) + Fe^{2+}(aq) -> Mg^{2+}(aq) + Fe(s)}\]

Magnesium changes from \(0\) to \(+2\), so it is oxidised and is the reducing agent. Iron changes from \(+2\) to \(0\), so \(\ce{Fe^{2+}}\) is reduced and is the oxidising agent.

Use the wording in full in explanations: “Mg loses two electrons, so its oxidation number increases from 0 to +2; Mg is oxidised and is the reducing agent.” This earns more credit than naming oxidation alone.

6.3 Disproportionation

A disproportionation reaction is a redox reaction in which the same element in one reactant is both oxidised and reduced.

For chlorine in cold aqueous sodium hydroxide:

\[\ce{Cl2 + 2OH^- -> Cl^- + ClO^- + H2O}\]

Chlorine changes from \(0\) to \(-1\) in \(\ce{Cl^-}\) and from \(0\) to \(+1\) in \(\ce{ClO^-}\), so chlorine disproportionates.

For hot aqueous sodium hydroxide, chlorate(V) is formed instead:

\[\ce{3Cl2 + 6OH^- -> 5Cl^- + ClO3^- + 3H2O}\]

The chlorine oxidation numbers are \(0\), \(-1\) and \(+5\).

Other common disproportionation examples include hydrogen peroxide: 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2. Oxygen changes from −1 in H2O2 to −2 in water and 0 in oxygen gas.

6.4 Balancing redox equations

Use the following sequence:

  1. assign oxidation numbers to the changing elements;
  2. identify the increase and decrease in oxidation number;
  3. multiply species so that total increase equals total decrease;
  4. balance the remaining atoms and then the charge using \(\ce{H2O}\), \(\ce{H+}\) or \(\ce{OH^-}\) as appropriate;
  5. check atoms and total charge on both sides.

For example, in acidic solution:

\[\ce{2MnO4^- + 5SO2 + 2H2O -> 2Mn^{2+} + 5SO4^{2-} + 4H+}\]

Manganese changes from \(+7\) to \(+2\) and sulfur changes from \(+4\) to \(+6\). The five-electron reduction of each manganese balances the two-electron oxidation of each sulfur.

For the half-equation method in acidic solution: balance atoms other than O and H, balance O with H2O, balance H with H+, then balance charge with electrons. Add the two half-equations, cancel electrons and any common species, then check both atoms and total charge. In alkaline solution, add OH− to both sides to remove H+ as water.

6.5 Halogens and reducing power

The oxidising power of the halogens decreases down Group 17:

\[\ce{F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2}\]

Therefore chlorine can oxidise bromide and iodide ions, but iodine cannot oxidise bromide ions:

\[\ce{Cl2 + 2I^- -> 2Cl^- + I2}\]

The reducing power of the halide ions increases down the group:

\[\ce{F^- < Cl^- < Br^- < I^-}\]

Larger halide ions have more shielding and their outer electron is further from the nucleus, so it is easier to remove.

Concentrated sulfuric acid is both an acid and an oxidising agent. It reacts with chloride ions mainly as an acid, but bromide and iodide ions are strong enough reducing agents to reduce sulfuric acid to sulfur dioxide, sulfur or hydrogen sulfide.

For a halogen displacement, write the two half-equations to prove electron transfer. For Cl2 + 2I− → 2Cl− + I2, iodine ions lose electrons and are oxidised; chlorine gains electrons and is reduced. The colour change is brown/orange iodine in aqueous solution, or purple iodine in an organic solvent/vapour depending on conditions.

Question 1 · Oxidation and reduction definitions · 1.6 Choice Easy Q1

Which row correctly describes oxidation and reduction in terms of electron transfer and changes in oxidation state?

Show explanation

B. Oxidation is loss of electrons and an increase in oxidation number; reduction is gain of electrons and a decrease.

Question 2 · Oxidation state of magnesium sulfate · 1.6 Choice Easy Q2

What is the oxidation state of \(\ce{MgSO4}\)?

Show explanation

D. A neutral compound has an overall oxidation state of zero: \(+2+6+4(-2)=0\).

Question 3 · Common oxidation numbers · 1.6 Choice Easy Q3

Which row describes the most common oxidation numbers of fluorine, oxygen and hydrogen in compounds?

Show explanation

D. Fluorine is \(-1\), oxygen is usually \(-2\) and hydrogen is usually \(+1\).

Question 4 · Chlorate disproportionation · 1.6 Choice Easy Q4

Chlorate(I) ions decompose on heating. Which row gives the oxidation states of chlorine in \(\ce{ClO^-}\), \(\ce{ClO3^-}\) and \(\ce{Cl^-}\)?

Show explanation

A. In \(\ce{ClO^-}\) chlorine is \(+1\); in \(\ce{ClO3^-}\) it is \(+5\); and in \(\ce{Cl^-}\) it is \(-1\).

Question 5 · Halogen displacement · 1.6 Choice Easy Q5

A student added a solution of iodine to a solution of sodium bromide. Which statement correctly describes what happens?

Show explanation

A. Iodine is below bromine in Group 17 and is not a strong enough oxidising agent to oxidise bromide ions.

Question 6 · Change in nitrogen oxidation number · 1.6 Choice Easy Q6

In which reaction is the change in oxidation number for nitrogen the smallest?

Show explanation

D. Nitrogen changes from \(+2\) in \(\ce{NO}\) to \(+4\) in \(\ce{NO2}\), a change of only 2.

Question 7 · Oxidation numbers in complex ions · 1.6 Choice Easy Q7

In the compound \(\ce{[ICl2]+[SbCl6]-}\), the oxidation number of chlorine is \(-1\). What are the oxidation numbers of I and Sb?

Show explanation

C. In \(\ce{[ICl2]+}\), I is \(+3\); in \(\ce{[SbCl6]-}\), Sb is \(+5\).

In which compound are there two different elements with the same oxidation number?

When sulfuric acid and sodium iodide react, one reaction is:

\[\ce{8NaI + 9H2SO4 -> 8NaHSO4 + 4I2 + H2S + 4H2O}\]

Which species has been oxidised?

A. Iodide changes from \(-1\) to \(0\) in \(\ce{I2}\).

In the reaction used to determine titanium dioxide in an ore, which element increases in oxidation number?

\[\ce{3TiO2 + 4BrF5 -> 3TiF4 + 2Br2 + 3O2}\]

Question 14 · Hydrogen as an oxidising agent · 1.6 Choice Medium Q7

In which reaction does hydrogen behave as an oxidising agent?

A. Hydrogen is reduced from \(0\) to \(-1\) in \(\ce{NaH}\), so it acts as the oxidising agent.

6 Electrochemistry - Multiple Choice: Hard

Question 15 · Ammonium nitrate decomposition · 1.6 Choice Hard Q1

When heated, ammonium nitrate decomposes:

\[\ce{NH4NO3 -> N2O + 2H2O}\]

The nitrogen atoms in \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) have different oxidation numbers. What are their changes in oxidation number?

Show explanation

A. Nitrogen changes from \(-3\) in \(\ce{NH4+}\) to \(+1\) in \(\ce{N2O}\), and from \(+5\) in \(\ce{NO3-}\) to \(+1\): changes are \(+4\) and \(-4\).

Question 16 · Disproportionation of HIO · 1.6 Choice Hard Q2

The acid \(\ce{HIO}\) disproportionates in aqueous solution:

\[\ce{qHIO -> rI2 + sHIO3 + tH2O}\]

The equation is balanced using oxidation numbers. What are the values of \(r\) and \(s\)?

Show explanation

C. The lowest whole-number equation is \(\ce{5HIO -> 2I2 + HIO3 + 2H2O}\).

Question 17 · Sulfur dioxide titration with iodine · 1.6 Choice Hard Q3

In winemaking, sulfur dioxide is titrated with iodine. What is the change in oxidation number of sulfur in this reaction?

Show explanation

B. Sulfur in \(\ce{SO2}\) is \(+4\) and is oxidised to \(+6\).

Question 18 · Vanadium and thiosulfate · 1.6 Choice Hard Q4

Using the redox potentials in the original question page, what is the lowest possible oxidation number to which vanadium(V) can be reduced by thiosulfate ions?

Show explanation

C. Only the \(\ce{VO2+ / VO^{2+}}\) to \(\ce{VO^{2+}}\) step gives a positive emf with thiosulfate, so the final vanadium oxidation state is \(+4\).

Question 19 · Oxidation of chlorine to chlorate(V) · 1.6 Choice Hard Q5

Which reagent and conditions are necessary to oxidise elemental chlorine into a compound containing chlorine in oxidation state \(+5\)?

Show explanation

D. Hot concentrated alkali gives chlorate(V), \(\ce{ClO3^-}\), in which chlorine is \(+5\).

Question 20 · Sulfuric acid and sodium halides · 1.6 Choice Hard Q6

Concentrated phosphoric acid gives a colourless acidic gas with sodium chloride and sodium iodide. Concentrated sulfuric acid gives a colourless acidic gas with sodium chloride but purple vapour with sodium iodide. Which deduction can be made?

Show explanation

B. Sulfuric acid oxidises iodide to iodine, whereas phosphoric acid does not; sulfuric acid is therefore the stronger oxidising agent than iodine in this comparison.

Structured questions

6 Electrochemistry - Structured Questions: Easy

Example 1 · Oxidation numbers of sulfur · 1.6 SQ Easy Q1

Complete the oxidation numbers of sulfur in \(\ce{SO2}\), \(\ce{SO3}\), \(\ce{H2SO4}\) and \(\ce{SF4}\). [4 marks]

(b) State the oxidation number of sulfur in elemental \(\ce{S8}\). [1 mark]

(c) In the reaction \(\ce{SnCl4 + 2Mg -> 2MgCl2 + Sn}\):

  • state the change in oxidation number of magnesium;
  • state the change in oxidation number of tin;
  • deduce which species is the reducing agent. [3 marks]

(d) Antimony reacts with nitric acid to form antimony oxide, nitrogen monoxide and water:

\[\ce{Sb + HNO3 -> Sb2O3 + NO + H2O}\]

State the change in oxidation number of antimony, the change in oxidation number of nitrogen, and identify the oxidising agent. [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) Sulfur is \(+4\) in \(\ce{SO2}\), \(+6\) in \(\ce{SO3}\), \(+6\) in \(\ce{H2SO4}\) and \(+4\) in \(\ce{SF4}\).
  • (b) \(0\).
  • (c) Mg: \(0\to+2\); Sn: \(+4\to0\); Mg is the reducing agent because it is oxidised.
  • (d) Sb: \(0\to+3\); N: \(+5\to+2\); nitric acid is the oxidising agent because it is reduced.

Example 2 · Reduction and oxidation numbers · 1.6 SQ Easy Q2

(a) State two definitions of reduction. [2 marks]

(b) Complete the oxidation numbers in the following species: \(\ce{S^{2-}}\), \(\ce{Pb^{2+}}\), \(\ce{V^{3+}}\), \(\ce{Si}\), \(\ce{Sb^{3+}}\) and \(\ce{H^-}\). [3 marks]

(c) Complete the oxidation numbers in:

  • copper in copper(I) oxide;
  • iron in iron(III) chloride;
  • tin in tin(IV) chloride;
  • vanadium in vanadium(II) oxide. [3 marks]

(d) Explain what is meant by disproportionation, and state whether \(\ce{Cu^{2+}+Mg->Mg^{2+}+Cu}\) is a disproportionation reaction. [4 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) Reduction is gain of electrons and decrease in oxidation number.
  • (b) The oxidation numbers are \(-2\), \(+2\), \(+3\), \(0\), \(+3\) and \(-1\), respectively.
  • (c) The named oxidation numbers are \(+1\), \(+3\), \(+4\) and \(+2\).
  • (d) Disproportionation is when the same element in one reactant is both oxidised and reduced. The magnesium reaction is not disproportionation: Mg is oxidised and Cu is reduced, so different elements change.

Example 3 · Disproportionation reactions · 1.6 SQ Easy Q3

(a) Write a balanced equation for the disproportionation reaction of sulfuric acid with copper(I) oxide to form copper, copper sulfate and water. Identify the element that disproportionates. [2 marks]

(b) Write a balanced equation for the reaction of nitrogen dioxide with water to form nitric acid and nitrous acid, \(\ce{HNO2}\). Identify the element that disproportionates. [2 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) \(\ce{Cu2O + H2SO4 -> Cu + CuSO4 + H2O}\); copper changes from \(+1\) to \(0\) and \(+2\).
  • (b) \(\ce{2NO2 + H2O -> HNO3 + HNO2}\); nitrogen changes from \(+4\) to \(+5\) and \(+3\).

6 Electrochemistry - Structured Questions: Medium

Example 4 · Calcium and iron redox reactions · 1.6 SQ Medium Q1

When calcium reacts with iron(II) nitrate, a redox reaction occurs:

\[\ce{Ca + Fe(NO3)2 -> Ca(NO3)2 + Fe}\]

(a)(i) Explain in terms of electrons which species has been oxidised and which has been reduced. [2 marks]

(a)(ii) Use oxidation numbers to show which species acts as the reducing agent. [2 marks]

(b) Give the full systematic name for \(\ce{Fe(NO3)2}\). [1 mark]

(c) For \(\ce{Co2O3 + 6HCl -> 2CoCl2 + 3H2O + Cl2}\), explain what has been reduced and whether the reaction is disproportionation. [5 marks]

(d) Explain whether \(\ce{MgO + HNO3 -> Mg(NO3)2 + H2O}\) is a redox reaction. [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) Ca loses two electrons and is oxidised; \(\ce{Fe^{2+}}\) gains two electrons and is reduced. Ca changes \(0\to+2\), so Ca is the reducing agent.
  • (b) Iron(II) nitrate.
  • (c) Co changes from \(+3\) to \(+2\) and is reduced. Chlorine remains \(-1\) in HCl and \(\ce{CoCl2}\) before forming \(\ce{Cl2}\), but this is not disproportionation because one reactant element is not both oxidised and reduced in the same species.
  • (d) It is not redox: Mg remains \(+2\), N remains \(+5\), H remains \(+1\) and O remains \(-2\).

Example 5 · Sulfur dichloride and disproportionation · 1.6 SQ Medium Q2

Under suitable conditions, \(\ce{SCl2}\) reacts with water to produce a yellow precipitate of sulfur and a solution containing \(\ce{SO2(aq)}\) and compound B.

(a) What is the oxidation number of sulfur in \(\ce{SCl2}\)? [1 mark]

(b) Deduce how sulfur’s oxidation number changes and identify the type of reaction. [4 marks]

(c) Suggest the identity of B and construct an equation for the reaction between \(\ce{SCl2}\) and water. [2 marks]

(d) State the observations when \(\ce{AgNO3(aq)}\) and acidified \(\ce{K2Cr2O7(aq)}\) are added to separate samples of solution A. Give the systematic name of \(\ce{KMnO4}\). [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) Sulfur is \(+2\) because \(x+2(-1)=0\).
  • (b) Sulfur changes from \(+2\) to \(+4\) in \(\ce{SO2}\) and from \(+2\) to \(0\) in sulfur. It is both oxidised and reduced, so this is disproportionation.
  • (c) B is HCl; \(\ce{2SCl2+2H2O->S+SO2+4HCl}\).
  • (d) \(\ce{AgNO3}\) gives a white precipitate of \(\ce{AgCl}\); acidified dichromate turns from orange to green; \(\ce{KMnO4}\) is potassium manganate(VII).

Example 6 · Balancing redox equations · 1.6 SQ Medium Q3

For each equation, write oxidation numbers for the changing elements and balance the equation by adding coefficients.

(a)(i)

\[\ce{MnO4^- + SO2 + H2O -> Mn^{2+} + SO4^{2-} + H+}\]

(a)(ii)

\[\ce{Cr2O7^{2-} + NO2^- + H+ -> Cr^{3+} + NO3^- + H2O}\]

(b) State what is observed in the reaction in part (a)(ii). [2 marks]

(c) Zinc displaces copper from copper sulfate. Write the balanced equation and explain, using oxidation numbers, why it is redox. [3 marks]

(d) Use a half-equation to explain which species is the oxidising reagent in part (c). [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a)(i) Mn \(+7\to+2\), S \(+4\to+6\); \(\ce{2MnO4^-+5SO2+2H2O->2Mn^{2+}+5SO4^{2-}+4H+}\).
  • (a)(ii) Cr \(+6\to+3\), N \(+3\to+5\); \(\ce{Cr2O7^{2-}+3NO2^-+8H+->2Cr^{3+}+3NO3^-+4H2O}\).
  • (b) Orange dichromate is reduced to green \(\ce{Cr^{3+}}\).
  • (c) \(\ce{Zn+CuSO4->ZnSO4+Cu}\); Zn \(0\to+2\) is oxidised and Cu \(+2\to0\) is reduced.
  • (d) \(\ce{Cu^{2+}+2e^- -> Cu}\); \(\ce{Cu^{2+}}\) gains electrons and is the oxidising agent.

Example 7 · Chlorine and sodium hypochlorite · 1.6 SQ Medium Q4

Household bleach contains \(\ce{NaClO}\).

(a) State the conditions required to produce \(\ce{NaClO}\) from chlorine and sodium hydroxide, give its systematic name, and give the formula of sodium chlorate(V). [3 marks]

(b)

\[\ce{Cl2(aq)+2NaOH(aq)->NaClO(aq)+NaCl(aq)+H2O(l)}\]

Give the ionic equation and explain, using oxidation numbers, why it is disproportionation. [4 marks]

(c) In \(\ce{NaClO(aq)+H2O2(aq)->NaCl(aq)+O2(g)+H2O(l)}\), explain why chlorine has been reduced. [1 mark]

(d) An excess of hydrogen peroxide is added to \(10.0\ \mathrm{cm^3}\) of bleach and \(108\ \mathrm{cm^3}\) of oxygen is collected. The molar gas volume is \(24.0\ \mathrm{dm^3\ mol^{-1}}\). Calculate the moles of oxygen, moles of \(\ce{NaClO}\), the concentration of \(\ce{NaClO}\), and the concentration in g per \(100\ \mathrm{cm^3}\). [5 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) Cold aqueous sodium hydroxide; sodium chlorate(I) or sodium hypochlorite; \(\ce{NaClO3}\).
  • (b) \(\ce{Cl2+2OH^- -> Cl^-+ClO^-+H2O}\); chlorine changes from \(0\) to \(-1\) and \(+1\), so it is both reduced and oxidised.
  • (c) Chlorine changes from \(+1\) in \(\ce{ClO^-}\) to \(-1\) in \(\ce{Cl^-}\).
  • (d) \(n(\ce{O2})=0.108/24.0=0.00450\ \mathrm{mol}\); \(n(\ce{NaClO})=0.00450\ \mathrm{mol}\); concentration \(=0.00450/0.0100=0.450\ \mathrm{mol\ dm^{-3}}\); mass concentration \(=0.450(74.5)(0.100)=3.35\ \mathrm{g\ per\ 100\ cm^3}\).

6 Electrochemistry - Structured Questions: Hard

Example 8 · Tetrathionate and iodine · 1.6 SQ Hard Q1

The tetrathionate ion is \(\ce{S4O6^{2-}}\) and is shown in Fig. 1.1.

(a) Calculate the oxidation number of sulfur in the tetrathionate ion and explain your answer. [2 marks]

(b) Sodium tetrathionate can be formed from sodium thiosulfate and iodine.

  • suggest an equation for the reaction;
  • identify the oxidising agent;
  • describe the observation showing that the reaction is complete. [4 marks]

(c) Sodium tetrathionate can also be made from sodium bisulfite and disulfur dichloride:

\[\ce{2NaHSO3 + S2Cl2 -> Na2S4O6 + 2HCl}\]

Explain whether this reaction is disproportionation, referring to sulfur oxidation numbers. [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) The average sulfur oxidation number is \(+2.5\): \(4x+6(-2)=-2\). The structure contains two sulfur atoms at \(+5\) and two inner sulfur atoms at \(0\), so the average need not be an integer.
  • (b) \(\ce{2Na2S2O3+I2->Na2S4O6+2NaI}\); iodine is the oxidising agent; the iodine colour disappears, or the starch blue-black colour disappears.
  • (c) No. Sulfur in \(\ce{NaHSO3}\) is \(+4\) and sulfur in \(\ce{S2Cl2}\) is \(+1\); one sulfur-containing reactant is oxidised and the other is reduced. Disproportionation requires the same reactant species to undergo both changes.

Example 9 · Photochromic glass · 1.6 SQ Hard Q2

Photochromic glass contains copper(I) chloride and silver(I) chloride. Under sunlight, silver(I) chloride separates into ions; chloride ions become chlorine atoms and silver(I) ions become silver atoms. Silver atoms cluster and darken the lenses.

(a) Write equations for the processes involved and state whether each is oxidation or reduction. [3 marks]

(b) Explain why a driver may not receive the full benefit of photochromic sunglasses on a cold, bright day. [2 marks]

(c) Write two equations showing how copper(I) chloride reacts with the products to remove silver atoms and lighten the lens. [2 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • (a) \(\ce{AgCl -> Ag+ + Cl^-}\); \(\ce{2Cl^- -> 2Cl + 2e^-}\) is oxidation; \(\ce{Ag+ + e^- -> Ag}\) is reduction.
  • (b) The darkening reaction is slower at low temperature, so the glass may not darken fully before the light is reduced by the windscreen or surroundings.
  • (c) \(\ce{Cu+ + Ag -> Cu + Ag+}\) and \(\ce{Ag+ + Cl^- -> AgCl}\).

Example 10 · Products of iodine and sulfuric acid reactions · 1.6 SQ Hard Q3

The reaction of sodium iodide with concentrated sulfuric acid forms a variety of products. Complete the table by identifying whether each product is formed by oxidation, reduction or neither: \(\ce{NaHSO4}\), \(\ce{SO2}\), \(\ce{Na2SO4}\), \(\ce{I2}\), \(\ce{HI}\), \(\ce{S}\), \(\ce{H2O}\) and \(\ce{H2S}\).

Sulfur dioxide also reacts with copper(II) chloride:

\[\ce{SO2 + 2H2O + 2CuCl2 -> H2SO4 + 2HCl + 2CuCl}\]

Identify the reducing agent and oxidising agent. [5 marks]

Sodium iodate reacts with sodium hydrogen sulfite:

\[\ce{2IO3^- + 5HSO3^- -> 3HSO4^- + 2SO4^{2-} + I2 + H2O}\]

Explain why sodium does not appear in the ionic equation and explain the role of hydrogen sulfite ions. [3 marks]

Show mark scheme / model answer
  • Table: \(\ce{NaHSO4}\) neither; \(\ce{SO2}\) reduction; \(\ce{Na2SO4}\) neither; \(\ce{I2}\) oxidation; \(\ce{HI}\) neither; \(\ce{S}\) reduction; \(\ce{H2O}\) neither; \(\ce{H2S}\) reduction.
  • Reducing and oxidising agents: \(\ce{SO2}\) is the reducing agent because sulfur changes \(+4\to+6\); \(\ce{Cu^{2+}}\) is the oxidising agent because copper changes \(+2\to+1\).
  • Ionic equation: sodium is a spectator ion, so it is omitted. \(\ce{HSO3^-}\) is the reducing agent; sulfur changes from \(+4\) to \(+6\) in sulfate or hydrogen sulfate.
