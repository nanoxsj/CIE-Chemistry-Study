14 Hydrocarbons

Alkanes, alkenes, crude oil and reactions

14 Hydrocarbons

本章只覆盖 AS 3.2 Hydrocarbons：alkanes、alkenes、crude oil、cracking、combustion、free-radical substitution、electrophilic addition、oxidation 和 addition polymerisation。题目均来自 3.2 Hydrocarbons 原题；不能独立提取的题图未使用截图替代。

Learning objectives

  • explain alkane unreactivity and free-radical substitution;
  • describe fractional distillation, cracking and combustion of hydrocarbon fuels;
  • apply electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes;
  • use bromine water and manganate(VII) reactions to identify C=C;
  • explain addition polymerisation and environmental effects of vehicle emissions.

14.1 Alkanes and free-radical substitution

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula CnH2n+2. They are relatively unreactive because C–C and C–H bonds have high bond enthalpies and are almost non-polar. They undergo combustion and, with halogens under UV light, free-radical substitution.

For chlorination of methane:

Initiation: Cl2 → 2Cl·
Propagation: Cl· + CH4 → HCl + CH3·
Propagation: CH3· + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl·
Termination: CH3· + CH3· → C2H6

Initiation makes radicals, propagation uses and regenerates a radical, and termination removes radicals. Homolytic fission splits the bonding pair equally: Cl–Cl → 2Cl·.

14.2 Crude oil, fractionation and cracking

Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons, mainly alkanes. Fractional distillation separates fractions by boiling point: larger molecules have stronger London forces and higher boiling points. A fraction contains hydrocarbons with a range of chain lengths, not one pure compound.

Cracking breaks larger saturated hydrocarbons into smaller, more useful molecules, producing alkanes and alkenes. It is carried out by vaporising the fraction and passing it over a hot catalyst such as alumina/silica, or by strong heating. It meets demand for small fuel molecules and for alkene feedstock. For example:

C8H18 → C5H12 + C3H6

14.3 Alkenes and electrophilic addition

Alkenes contain C=C and have general formula CnH2n for an open-chain molecule with one double bond. The π bond is electron-rich and weaker than the σ bond, so it reacts with electrophiles. In electrophilic addition, the alkene first donates π electrons to an electrophile, forming a carbocation; a negative ion then bonds to the positive carbon.

Reagent and conditions Product / observation
Br2 in organic solvent dibromoalkane; bromine decolourises
Br2(aq) bromoalcohol and dibromoalkane
steam, phosphoric acid catalyst, 600 K, high pressure alcohol
HBr bromoalkane; unsymmetrical alkenes may give major/minor products
H2, Ni, heat alkane
cold dilute acidified KMnO4 vicinal diol; purple decolourises
hot concentrated acidified KMnO4 oxidative cleavage of C=C

The more stable carbocation is formed more readily; this explains the major product in addition of HBr to an unsymmetrical alkene.

14.4 Combustion, emissions and catalytic converters

Complete combustion has excess oxygen and makes CO2 and H2O. Incomplete combustion, when oxygen is limited, produces CO and/or carbon. Carbon monoxide binds strongly to haemoglobin and reduces oxygen transport. High-temperature engines also form NOx; NOx and unburnt hydrocarbons contribute to photochemical smog.

Catalytic converters use redox reactions to reduce NOx to N2 and oxidise CO/hydrocarbons to CO2 and H2O, for example 2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2.

14.5 Addition polymerisation

In addition polymerisation, many alkene monomers join when the C=C opens, forming only C–C σ bonds in the backbone. The repeat unit is shown in brackets with continuation bonds, for example poly(propene): [–CH2–CH(CH3)–]n. No small molecule is eliminated.

Multiple choice questions

Question 1 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q1

Which equation represents a valid propagation step in the free radical reaction between ethane and chlorine?

Show explanation

B. It consumes one radical and forms one radical. D is termination because radicals are destroyed.

Question 2 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q2

A fraction of distilled crude oil contains molecules with between 15 and 19 carbon atoms. This fraction is cracked by strong heating. Why is this done?
1 To produce alkenes. 2 To produce smaller molecules which are in higher demand. 3 To insert oxygen atoms into the hydrocarbons.

Show explanation

B. Cracking produces smaller hydrocarbons including alkenes; it does not insert oxygen.

Question 3 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q3

Ethene reacts with steam in the presence of sulfuric acid: C2H4 + H2O → CH3CH2OH. What type of reaction is this?

Show explanation

B. H and OH add across the C=C.

Question 4 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q4

Which statements about alkenes are correct?
1 They are less reactive than alkanes towards electrophiles. 2 They are used as monomers for polymerisation. 3 They are formed when higher alkanes are cracked.

Show explanation

C. Alkenes are more reactive towards electrophiles because of their π bond.

Question 5 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q5

One catalytic-converter reaction is between nitrogen oxide and octane. Which equation gives only non-toxic products?

Show explanation

D. It is balanced and gives CO2, N2 and water, rather than toxic CO.

Question 6 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q6

When bromine reacts with propene in an organic solvent at room temperature, what is the mechanism by which bromine attacks the propene?

Show explanation

A. The alkene π bond attacks polarised Br2.

Question 7 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q7

Which monomer could produce a polymer with repeating unit –CH2–CH(Cl)–?

Show explanation

B. Addition polymerisation opens the double bond while retaining the Cl substituent.

Question 8 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q8

Four students made statements about alkanes and alkenes. W: bromine reacts with alkanes by electrophilic substitution. X: bromine reacts with alkenes by a free-radical addition mechanism. Y: alkenes can be oxidised by acidified manganate(VII) ions. Z: alkenes are formed from alkanes by cracking. Which two students are correct?

Show explanation

D. Alkane halogenation is free-radical substitution; alkene bromination is electrophilic addition.

Question 9 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q9

The hydrocarbon C17H36 can be cracked. Which compound is least likely to be produced?

Show explanation

D. This would leave only CH2, not a realistic stable hydrocarbon co-product.

Question 10 · 3.2 Choice Easy Q10

In ultraviolet light, ethane and chlorine react to give a mixture of products. Which compound could be present?

Show explanation

C. Two ethyl radicals can combine in a termination step: C2H5· + C2H5· → C4H10.

Question 11 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q1

The cracking of one CnH2n+2 molecule produces two hydrocarbon molecules only. Each contains the same number of carbon atoms and has non-cyclic structural isomers. What is n?

Show explanation

B. Each product must have four carbons (the first chain length with structural isomers), so the parent has eight.

Question 12 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q2

Which reaction would not give propene?

Show explanation

B. Aqueous OH− gives substitution to propan-2-ol; ethanolic OH− favours elimination.

Question 13 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q3

Ethene reacts with aqueous bromine to give CH2BrCH2Br and CH2BrCH2OH. Which statement about these products is correct?

Show explanation

C. Hydrolysis of either can give ethane-1,2-diol; only the bromoalcohol itself has an O–H bond.

Question 14 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q4

Which hydrocarbon would not be collected on heating pentane over aluminium oxide?

Show explanation

D. A cracking reaction cannot make a ten-carbon product from pentane.

Question 15 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q5

Pent-2-ene reacts with cold, dilute, acidified manganate(VII) ions. What is produced in the greatest amount?

Show explanation

A. Mild oxidation adds an OH group to each carbon of C=C, forming a diol.

Question 16 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q8

Which alkenes, on reaction with steam at 600 K and high pressure in the presence of phosphoric acid, could produce an alcohol containing a chiral carbon atom?
1 (CH3)2C=CH2 2 CH3CH=CHCH3 3 CH3CH2CH=CH2

Show explanation

C. Hydration of but-2-ene or but-1-ene can give butan-2-ol with four different groups at C2.

Question 17 · 3.2 Choice Medium Q9

0.200 mol of a hydrocarbon undergoes complete combustion to give 35.2 g CO2 and 14.4 g H2O. What is its molecular formula?

Show explanation

D. n(CO2)=35.2/44.0=0.800 mol, giving 0.800/0.200=4 carbon atoms. n(H2O)=14.4/18.0=0.800 mol, giving 2×0.800/0.200=8 hydrogen atoms.

Question 18 · 3.2 Choice Hard Q3

Acrylic acid is produced from propene. Which statement about acrylic acid is not correct?

Show explanation

C. A carboxylic acid does not contain an aldehyde or ketone carbonyl required for the 2,4-DNPH test.

Question 19 · 3.2 Choice Hard Q6

Alkane X has formula C4H10. X reacts with Cl2 in sunlight to produce only two monochloroalkanes. Both form the same alkene Y, and no other organic products, with hot ethanolic KOH. What is produced when Y is treated with hot concentrated acidified KMnO4?

Show explanation

B. X is 2-methylpropane and Y is 2-methylpropene. Oxidative cleavage gives propanone and CO2.

Question 20 · 3.2 Choice Hard Q7

A possible polymer has repeat unit –CH2–CH(OH)–. By which method might this polymer be made?

Show explanation

C. Hydrolysis replaces Cl by OH on the polymer chain.

Structured questions

Question 1 · 3.2 SQ Easy Q1(a-d)

(a) State two reasons why alkanes are unreactive.

(b)(i) State what is meant by a radical. (ii) State the condition required to form chlorine free radicals from Cl2.

(c) State the type of bond fission involved.

(d)(i) Chloromethane is formed from methane and chlorine. Write equations for the two propagation steps.

(d)(ii) Write an equation to show how ethane can be formed.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. C–C/C–H bonds are strong (high bond enthalpy) and almost non-polar.
  2. A radical is a species with an unpaired electron. UV light is required.
  3. Homolytic fission: each atom takes one electron from the covalent bond.
  4. Cl· + CH4 → HCl + CH3·; CH3· + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl·.
  5. CH3· + CH3· → C2H6; this is a termination step.

Question 2 · 3.2 SQ Easy Q2(b-d)

Propene reacts with hydrogen bromide. (a) Use curly arrows to complete the mechanism for formation of the major product. (b) Draw the displayed formula of the minor product. (c) Explain why there is a major and minor product.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. The π bond attacks Hδ+ of HBr and the H–Br bond breaks to Br−; then Br− attacks the carbocation. The major product is 2-bromopropane.
  2. The minor product is 1-bromopropane, CH3CH2CH2Br.
  3. Protonation can form either carbocation. The secondary carbocation is more stable than the primary carbocation, so the route to 2-bromopropane is favoured.

Question 3 · 3.2 SQ Easy Q3(a-d)

Alkanes undergo complete and incomplete combustion. State the difference, including a balanced symbol equation for each, using octane. Carbon monoxide is emitted in exhaust fumes. (i) State why it is dangerous. (ii) State another pollutant released in car exhaust fumes and its atmospheric effect.

(c)(i) Octane can be cracked to form pentane and one other product. Give the balanced equation.

(c)(ii) Describe how catalytic cracking is carried out.

(d) Describe a chemical test that distinguishes the two products.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Complete combustion uses sufficient O2: 2C8H18 + 25O2 → 16CO2 + 18H2O. Incomplete combustion has limited O2, for example 2C8H18 + 17O2 → 16CO + 18H2O.
  2. CO binds strongly to haemoglobin, reducing oxygen transport in the blood.
  3. NOx contributes to acid rain/photochemical smog; unburnt hydrocarbons contribute to photochemical smog.
  4. C8H18 → C5H12 + C3H6; vaporise and pass over hot alumina/silica.
  5. Bromine water: propene decolourises it; pentane shows no change without UV light.

Question 4 · 3.2 SQ Medium Q1(a-d)

A reaction scheme involving cyclohexane includes free-radical substitution with chlorine, followed by conversion to cyclohexene. (a) State the essential condition for reaction 1. (b) Give the key initiation, propagation and termination equations. (c) Deduce the type of reaction in reaction 2. (d) Hex-3-ene is converted to propanoic acid: state reagents and conditions.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. UV light/sunlight.
  2. Initiation: Cl2 → 2Cl·. Propagation: Cl· + C6H12 → HCl + C6H11·; C6H11· + Cl2 → C6H11Cl + Cl·. Termination: two radicals combine.
  3. Elimination (dehydrohalogenation).
  4. Hot concentrated acidified KMnO4; oxidative cleavage of the central C=C produces two propanoic-acid molecules.

Question 5 · 3.2 SQ Medium Q2(b-d)

(a) An alcohol reacts with concentrated H3PO4 to form only pent-2-ene. Give the formula of the alcohol and name the reaction type. (b) Pent-2-ene reacts with HBr to form two bromoalkanes: show the mechanism for 2-bromopentane. (c) 1.33 g pent-1-ene is warmed to 60 °C in a 500 cm3 sealed container. Calculate the pressure.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Pentan-3-ol, CH3CH2CH(OH)CH2CH3; elimination/dehydration.
  2. Electrophilic addition: π electrons attack Hδ+, H–Br breaks to Br−, then Br− attacks the carbocation.
  3. n = 1.33/70.0 = 0.0190 mol; T = 333 K, V = 5.00×10−4 m3. p = nRT/V = 0.0190×8.31×333/(5.00×10−4) = 1.05×105 Pa.

Question 6 · 3.2 SQ Medium Q3(a-d)

(a) What is meant by cracking and why is it carried out? (b)(i) State two conditions required for cracking alkanes. (b)(ii) Construct a balanced equation for the formation of heptane and two other products by cracking tetradecane.

An alkane used in gasoline undergoes combustion producing CO, oxides of nitrogen and unburnt hydrocarbons.

(c)(i) Explain why CO is dangerous.

(c)(ii) Describe the environmental effect of oxides of nitrogen and unburnt hydrocarbons.

(d) Use equations to show how a catalytic converter reduces CO and NOx.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Cracking is thermal decomposition of large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller hydrocarbons. It is carried out to meet demand for smaller fuels and alkenes.
  2. High temperature and a catalyst (or strong heating in thermal cracking).
  3. One valid equation is C14H30 → C7H16 + C4H8 + C3H6.
  4. CO forms a stable complex with haemoglobin, preventing blood from carrying enough oxygen.
  5. NOx and hydrocarbons react in sunlight to form photochemical smog; NOx also forms acids in rain.
  6. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2; 2CO + O2 → 2CO2.

Question 7 · 3.2 SQ Medium Q4(a-d)

Propene and propane can be made by cracking hexane. (a) Explain why propane is relatively unreactive. (b) Describe a test to distinguish propane and propene. (c) State the products of propene with steam/acid catalyst, cold dilute acidified KMnO4, and bromine water. (d) Draw poly(propene), showing two repeat units.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Propane has strong C–C/C–H σ bonds with low polarity, so much energy is needed to break bonds and it has no electron-rich π bond.
  2. Add bromine water: propene decolourises it; propane remains orange/brown under these conditions.
  3. Steam gives propan-2-ol; cold dilute acidified KMnO4 gives propane-1,2-diol; bromine water gives 1-bromopropan-2-ol and 1,2-dibromopropane.
  4. Correct repeat units are –CH2–CH(CH3)–CH2–CH(CH3)– within continuation bonds.

Question 8 · 3.2 SQ Hard Q1(a-d)

(a)(i) 1,2-dibromoethane reacts with bromine in UV light. Write an equation for the initiation step.

(a)(ii) Explain why this is homolytic fission.

(b) Write two propagation equations producing 1,1,2-tribromoethane.

(c) Write equations showing formation of 1,2,3,4-tetrabromobutane and hexabromoethane.

(d) Suggest limitations of free-radical substitution.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Br2 → 2Br· under UV light.
  2. One electron from Br–Br goes to each Br atom, making two radicals.
  3. Br· removes H, then the carbon radical reacts with Br2; show correct radical chain equations.
  4. Radical coupling gives higher-mass products; continued substitution gives heavily brominated products.
  5. It gives a mixture of products, may substitute at more than one position and makes separation difficult.

Question 9 · 3.2 SQ Hard Q2(a-c)

(a) 10 cm3 of gaseous hydrocarbon CxHy burns in excess oxygen. After absorbing CO2 with NaOH, use gas volumes to calculate CO2 volume, O2 used and deduce x, y and z.

(b) Branched C4H8 reacts with HBr to give two C4H9Br isomers. Give structures/names and explain why one product dominates.

(c) Draw the mechanism for pent-2-ene with HBr.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. CO2 volume is 20 cm3; calculate O2 used from the initial/final gas volumes and balance using volume ratios.
  2. W is 2-methylpropene. Addition gives 2-bromo-2-methylpropane as the major product through the more stable tertiary carbocation, plus 1-bromo-2-methylpropane.
  3. Show electrophilic addition: π electrons to H of HBr, C–H bond formation/carbocation, then Br− attack.

Question 10 · 3.2 SQ Hard Q3(a-b)

(a) A student titrates cooking oil with bromine water to determine the number of C=C bonds. Suggest two improvements for more consistent results.

(b) Use the titration data, Mr, density and drop volume to deduce the number of C=C double bonds in one molecule of the oil.

Show mark scheme / model answer
  1. Use a fixed measured volume/mass of oil rather than drops; repeat titrations and calculate a mean; use a consistent endpoint and accurate volumetric apparatus.
  2. Find mean moles of Br2, calculate moles of oil from mass/Mr, then use the 1:1 alkene:Br2 ratio to find the number of C=C bonds.
